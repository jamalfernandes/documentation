## Settled: we keep the name

Romain Cristofini's practice uses _"ecologie humaine et essentielle."_ You are _L'Ecologie Humaine._ We considered renaming and **decided to keep the name.**

It is yours, it's the mother-concept the whole house hangs from, and changing it to avoid a competitor would be the same compromise that produced HBA in the first place.

That decision is settled. What follows from it is not — so here are the four rules it commits us to.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Own la juste place as the concept, not the category name">
    "Ecologie humaine" is contested ground now, and no amount of copy wins it outright. _La juste place_ is uncontested, it's yours, and it's more useful anyway — a specific idea rather than a category label. The name goes on the door; _la juste place_ is what shows up in the writing, the diagnostic, the offer names and the site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Don't fight on his keywords">
    _Leaders eclaires, intelligence spirituelle, leadership regeneratif_ — he holds these, with a 4,700-member community feeding them. Chasing them means competing at his strength with none of his volume. Build on what's distinctly yours instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let un nouveau design de societe carry the differentiation">
    It's already your show title, and it's the sharper end of the brand — it says what the ecology is _for_. Where the name overlaps, the tagline separates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Promote scaling humain">
    It's already your job title on your own site. It's business-native, no French competitor uses it, and it translates the whole worldview into two words a founder already understands. Promote it from a title to a working term.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **The upside:** you keep the concept the entire house rests on, and you avoid the thing that broke the last version of this brand. **The cost we accept:** shared search ground with your nearest competitor. Rules 1–3 are how we manage it.
</Note>
