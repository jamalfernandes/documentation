## The sidewalk rule

Your own image, and it governs everything.

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="Front door" icon="door-closed">
    Teaching spirituality: off. No content whose job is to explain consciousness, energy or awakening. You said you don't want to teach it again. We don't.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Sidewalk" icon="footprints">
    Present, not preached. This is the default. The worldview shows up in how you read a company, what you ask, what you build, and the calm you carry. A spiritual truth arrives dressed as a business insight.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Private" icon="lock">
    Held. Your own transformation, and your son. Yours to share on purpose, never as content fuel.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Warning>
  The test, before you publish anything: am I demonstrating this, or explaining it? Demonstrating is the sidewalk. Explaining is the front door.
</Warning>

## Keep, translate, hold

The table you check when you're unsure a word will land.

| Your word | In business | Lane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collective soul | Culture | Translate |
| Spiritual crisis | Lack of humanity, loss of meaning | Translate |
| Alignment / misalignment | Already clear in business, and it's your signature | **Keep** |
| The living, _le vivant_ | A living system, a healthy ecosystem | Translate |
| Right place, _la juste place_ | Your heart concept, and it translates itself | **Keep** |
| Regeneration | Growth that lasts, a system that renews itself | Translate |
| Interconnection, interdependence | How the parts of a system affect each other | Translate |
| Consciousness, awareness | Clarity, seeing what is really going on | Translate |
| Energy | Momentum, where the life is in the business | Translate |
| Soul (of a person) | Vision, what you are really here for | Translate |
| Oneness, unity, awakening, meditation |  | **Hold** (sidewalk or private only) |

The science bridge. When a spiritual idea has a business-accepted twin, use the twin. Collective consciousness becomes groupthink, herd behaviour, culture. You say the true thing in words a CEO already trusts, and you go deeper only if they lean in.

<Note>
  Never delete. The old draft put your vocabulary on an avoid list. That was the mistake that made you feel misaligned. The rule is translate or sidewalk, never delete.
</Note>

## Message hierarchy: the four levels

Every post, email and conversation lives on one of these. Most should live on level 1 or 2.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Level 1: Hook (sidewalk)" icon="message-circle">
    Universal, no jargon, sorts people for themselves.

    > "Your company is a living system. When something in it is out of place, you feel it before you can name it."
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Level 2: Diagnosis" icon="stethoscope">
    Tension, conflict, burnout and loss of meaning are symptoms, not the problem. The problem is that someone or something is out of its right place.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Level 3: Method (HBA)" icon="route">
    You read the company through its leader, find where the human system is off, and put it back, so it can grow without breaking.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Level 4: Root" icon="anchor">
    Why it works: a company is a human ecosystem, and humans, systems and nature follow the same laws.

    Only for people who lean in. This is where the worldview gets named. Never open here.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The hook bank

Fifteen openers you can pull from. All level 1 or 2. All sidewalk.

1. The problem is almost never the person. It's the place.
2. You feel the tension before you can name it. That's information, not a flaw.
3. A company doesn't break all at once. It drifts, slowly.
4. People call me about a team conflict. We almost always end up somewhere else.
5. What you're calling a communication problem is usually a placement problem.
6. Growing without losing your soul isn't naïve. It's structural.
7. A leader's exhaustion is rarely a question of scheduling.
8. In a healthy ecosystem, nothing is surplus. The same is true of a company.
9. You hired the right person. You put them in the wrong place.
10. The day I stopped trying to fix the symptom, everything got simpler.
11. There's a difference between a company that runs and a company that's alive.
12. Complexity isn't a sign that you're growing. It's usually a sign that something has slipped out of place.
13. An isolated leader isn't a strong leader. It's a system with a hole in it.
14. You don't reorganise an ecosystem. You give it back its balance.
15. What nature has always known how to do, organisations are relearning.
