## What the LinkedIn tests are telling us

You've been running LinkedIn ads weekly to see which audience responds, targeting CEOs, founders and owners. Three or four-day runs. Here is what came back.

| Run | Impressions | Reached | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Larger | 11,500 | 8,500 | 49 profile views, **zero engagement** |
| Smaller | 7,500 | 5,900 | 3 link clicks, 3 post engagements, 2 newsletter signups |

<Note>
  The smaller run is the more interesting one, and the lesson is not the one it looks like. Less reach produced more action. That is what happens when spend stops being spread thinly across a broad audience. It is a signal about targeting, not about budget.
</Note>

Two honest readings, and we can't yet tell which is right:

- **49 profile views with zero engagement** is not nothing. People looked you up rather than engaging in the feed, which is exactly what a considered B2B audience does. LinkedIn engagement rate undercounts that.
- Equally, a boost-style buy on the most expensive inventory there is remains the wrong instrument, and the earlier show-replay boost produced views and nothing else.

</Info>

## The cost ladder

| Platform | Cost | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meta** (Facebook and Instagram) | Cheapest | Biggest audience, most engaged, cheapest reach |
| **Google and YouTube** | Middle | YouTube in particular, since you already have the content |
| **LinkedIn** | Most expensive | A narrow, high-value HBA campaign with real targeting. Not the boost button |

LinkedIn stays in the mix as a **test ground rather than a growth channel**. It is where your buyers actually are, so it is the fastest place to learn which message lands. Learn there, then buy reach on Meta where it costs a fraction.

## Bringing in help

You said paid ads are not your passion and you'd rather someone took them over. That's the right instinct, and you're already talking to one agency that approached you on LinkedIn plus another on the brand-positioning side.

Two things worth knowing before those calls:

- **Google and YouTube ads run from the same account.** If you're going to do both, one person or one small agency should hold it, rather than several people in the same account.
- **Ask them what they'd measure in month one.** Anyone who answers "reach" or "impressions" is selling you the LinkedIn boost again. The right answer is cost per link click, then what those clicks do.

## The honest caveat

<Warning>
  Ads amplify something that already converts. Right now nothing in your funnel is proven to convert. There's no lead page yet, and the show doesn't yet bring clients, in your words.

  So treat this phase as buying data, not buying clients. Once the questionnaire exists and we know what a visitor does, ads become an investment rather than a test.

  One caveat on that caveat: you now have a client who converted by reading the booklet and recognising himself in it. That is the first evidence of something in the funnel actually working, and it is why the questionnaire is the right thing to point spend at.
</Warning>
