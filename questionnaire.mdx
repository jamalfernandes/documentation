Decided on the 14 August call. This replaces the book preview as the primary lead magnet. Built out here in full, ready to go into Typeform.

## Why this one and not the book preview

The book preview asks someone to take an interest in you. The questionnaire asks them to look at themselves. Different transaction entirely, and it is the one that has already worked once: a client converted after reading a booklet and recognising their own situation in it. Nobody sold to them. They sold to themselves.

<Note>
  The mechanism, in one line. A leader who names their own symptom out loud has already done the first half of the diagnostic. Your only job is to be standing there when they finish.
</Note>

It also does something the book preview never could. It tells you, before the first conversation, which of the three readings a company needs. That is qualification for free.

## Working title

**Is your company in its right place?**

Twelve questions. Under three minutes. No score out of ten, no personality type, no grade.

<Info>
  Alternatives if that one does not sit right: _What is really going on in your company?_ or _Twelve questions most leaders answer badly._ The second gets more clicks and carries more risk. English here for approval. Nothing publishes until the French is signed off.
</Info>

## The opening screen

<Note>
  Most of what leaders bring me is not the actual problem. The conflict, the exhaustion, the person who has become impossible: those are symptoms. Underneath, something in the company has slipped out of its right place.

  Twelve questions. Three minutes. At the end you will see which of the three it most likely is in your case.

  There are no right answers, and nothing here is a judgement on you.
</Note>

That last line matters more than it looks. The respondent is a defended person being asked twelve uncomfortable questions. Take the judgement off the table in the first screen or they abandon at question four.

## The twelve questions

Same answer scale throughout, which keeps it fast: **Never · Sometimes · Often · Almost always**.

Three blocks of four. The respondent never sees the block names.

### Block A · The leader's place

| # | Question |
| --- | --- |
| A1 | Someone else in the company makes decisions that should be yours. |
| A2 | You do work that you know is not yours to be doing. |
| A3 | There is a subject you avoid raising with someone, because of how they will react. |
| A4 | You feel more like the person running the company than like its leader. |

### Block B · The frame

| # | Question |
| --- | --- |
| B1 | Two people believe the same responsibility is theirs. |
| B2 | You agree to things you wanted to refuse. |
| B3 | The same conflict comes back, in the same shape, with different people. |
| B4 | One person holds something the company would struggle to replace. |

### Block C · Growth and energy

| # | Question |
| --- | --- |
| C1 | The company has become more complicated without becoming clearer. |
| C2 | You finish the week without a single hour that was yours. |
| C3 | Decisions are waiting on you, and have been waiting a while. |
| C4 | You have a plan for the next stage that you have not started. |

<Warning>
  Notice what is not in here. No question about feelings, energy, alignment, consciousness or meaning. Every question describes something the respondent can see happening in their company this week. That is [the sidewalk rule](/messaging) applied to a diagnostic: the worldview is entirely in which twelve things got asked, and nowhere in the wording.
</Warning>

## Scoring

Never 0 · Sometimes 1 · Often 2 · Almost always 3. Each block scores out of 12, total out of 36.

Two readings come out of it, and they do different jobs.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="The highest block gives the profile" icon="compass">
    Where the misalignment sits. This is what the respondent sees, and what you use to prepare the call. Ties go to A, then B, then C, because the leader's place is upstream of everything else.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The total gives the urgency" icon="gauge">
    How loudly the system is asking. Internal mostly. The respondent gets it as a phrase, never as a number.
  </Card>
</Columns>

| Total | Band | Said as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 8 | Settled | "Your company is holding together well." |
| 9 to 17 | Drifting | "Something has started to move out of place." |
| 18 to 26 | Under strain | "Your system is spending real energy compensating." |
| 27 to 36 | Asking loudly | "This is not going to resolve on its own." |

<Info>
  No number goes on the result screen. A score out of 36 reads as a measurement, and the [case study rules](/proof) forbid announcing results that have not been measured. The same discipline applies here. Name the pattern, do not quantify the company.
</Info>

## The four results

Three profiles plus the honest one. Each result screen has the same four parts: the name, what it usually means, one anonymised echo, one next step.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Result 1 · The seat nobody is sitting in" icon="armchair">
    **When Block A is highest.**

    The leadership place in your company is not fully held. Perhaps you have not fully taken it, perhaps someone before you never fully left it, perhaps it emptied and was never refilled. The company does not stay empty for long. Somebody else moves in, usually without meaning to, and often without anyone noticing.

    _One case. A leader was about to dismiss an employee who had become impossible to work with. Coaching had already been tried, on both of them, and had changed nothing. The diagnostic found that the leadership function had been vacant since his co-founder died years earlier. He set the frame back. The dismissal was dropped, and the employee returned to their own job._

    **What to do with this.** Before changing anything about anyone else, get clear on what is actually yours to decide and what you have stopped deciding.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Result 2 · The frame that stopped holding" icon="frame">
    **When Block B is highest.**

    Roles overlap, limits are negotiable, and the same conflicts keep returning in new clothes. The instinct is to fix it with more process. Process is not what is missing. What is missing is a frame that everyone can feel, which is a different thing and comes from a different place.

    _One case. A family business of about twenty people: revenue slipping, departures multiplying, outside partners no longer quite respecting the company's rules, as though it had lost its centre of gravity. It did not need more procedures. It needed its leader to take their place again._

    **What to do with this.** Write down the three decisions that are genuinely yours. Notice how many of the recurring conflicts sit on top of one of them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Result 3 · Growth that has outrun its architecture" icon="trending-up">
    **When Block C is highest.**

    Your ambition is probably right. The problem is almost never the plan. It is that the human, managerial and financial architecture underneath it cannot yet carry it, and you are personally absorbing the difference. That is why you are tired, and why the next step keeps not starting.

    _One case. A leader wanted to buy several companies and build a group. The diagnostic confirmed the vision was sound, and that the order had to be respected. In the two months after, he hired the salesperson he had been replacing himself, promoted the people who were ready, and got the sequence back._

    **What to do with this.** Do not shrink the plan. Ask what would have to exist around you for the plan to be carried by something other than you.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Result 4 · Nothing much is out of place" icon="circle-check">
    **When the total is 8 or under.**

    On these twelve questions, your company is holding together. Places are reasonably clear, the frame holds, and growth is not costing you more than it should.

    Keep it. A company drifts slowly, and the moment worth watching is the next time you grow, because that is when places move and nobody notices.

    _No offer on this screen. Offer them the show and the weekly email instead._
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Result 4 must stay in. A questionnaire that tells everybody they have a problem is a sales instrument, and people can smell it. The one that occasionally says "you are fine" is the one the others believe. It will also keep your call calendar clean.
</Warning>

## The capture

Show the profile straight away. Ask for the email for the longer version. Giving first is the whole brand.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Result screen, free">
    Profile name, the paragraph, the anonymised echo, the next step. No email required to get here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The ask">
    "Want the longer read on this one? Three pages on what usually sits underneath it, what people try first that does not work, and what does. Enter your email and it is with you in a minute."
  </Step>
  <Step title="The consent line">
    "One idea a week, and you can leave whenever you want. No sales sequence."
  </Step>
  <Step title="The call, offered once">
    Below the email field, not instead of it: "If you would rather just talk it through, book thirty minutes." Some people will skip the PDF entirely, and those are the best ones.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **This is blocked until one thing changes.** Your site currently promises "Aucune liste de diffusion. Pas de relances commerciales." As written, the questionnaire would capture emails it is formally forbidden to use, which is the exact trap [the e-book already fell into](/emails).

  The wording in step 3 keeps the honesty and opens the door. It needs your decision, and it is the one thing holding this up. Everything else on this page can be built tomorrow.
</Warning>

## The three follow-up PDFs

One per profile, three pages each, and they mostly already exist. Each is the corresponding case from [the documented cases](/proof), written out properly, plus the pattern, plus what does not work. The case does the persuading. Anonymisation rules apply exactly as they do everywhere else.

## The follow-up email

Sent 48 hours later, once, to anyone who completed it.

<Note>
  Subject: _the part of your answers I keep thinking about_

  You answered twelve questions the other day and came out at **[profile]**.

  One thing worth adding. In almost every company I have read this way, the thing the leader originally called me about was not the thing that was wrong. It was the loudest symptom of something quieter and further upstream. Your answers point somewhere specific.

  If you want to know what I would look at first in your case, reply with one line about your company. I will tell you, whether or not anything comes of it.
</Note>

Ask for a reply, not a call. A reply costs nothing, and it starts the conversation on your side of the table.

## Build spec

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Tool | Typeform. One question per screen, progress bar visible, four-option scale |
| Length | 12 questions plus 1 result screen plus 1 capture screen |
| Logic | One jump only, on the result. Score three blocks, branch on the highest, override to Result 4 if the total is 8 or under |
| Placement | Embedded on the site, not a link out. Homepage and the HBA page |
| Fields captured | Email, first name, company size. Nothing else. Every extra field costs completions |
| Language | French at launch. English version second, for the YouTube audience arriving through auto-translate |
| Tracking | Completion rate, profile distribution, email opt-in rate, and calls booked, per profile |

<Info>
  Profile distribution is the number to watch. If nearly everyone lands on the same result, the questions are not separating and the blocks need rebalancing. If Result 1 dominates, that is not a fault: it would confirm what [the three cases and the ICP work](/audiences) are already suggesting, which is that the leader who does not quite feel like the leader is the real market.
</Info>

## Limits

<Warning>
  Same rules as everything else here.

  - It is a set of questions, never a diagnosis. Do not use the word.
  - No percentages, no benchmarks, no "companies like yours."
  - No promised outcome, and no timeline to one.
  - The cases stay anonymous, including in the PDFs.
  - Result 4 gets no offer.
</Warning>
