Three approaches to layout and information architecture. **These are structure, not design** — no colour, type or imagery decisions here. What changes between them is what a stranger meets first.

All three assume the same starting point: the site is already well written and already on-brand. None of them throw anything away.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="A — Reorder">
    ## Reorder the one-pager

    Keep the single scrolling page. Move the commercial door above the worldview, surface the proof, fix the stale block. **The smallest intervention that solves the real problems.**

    |  |  |
    | --- | --- |
    | Effort | Low — reordering what exists, plus one new page |
    | Best for | Shipping this month, with no rebuild |
    | Time to live | Days |

    ```text
      hero            worldview + "for leaders" line
      core message    la juste place
      four doors
      DIRIGEANTS      <- moved up
      THE LADDER      <- moved up, + price anchors
      proof strip     <- moved up from the very bottom
      lead magnet     fixed promise -> /diagnostic
      manifesto       teaser -> its own page
      other pillars   ere des femmes, education
      programmes      dates fixed
      about           reordered bio
      books, conferences, contact
    ```

    **What you gain** — ships fastest, nothing written gets thrown away, fixes all five problems, and keeps the worldview-led approach exactly as agreed.

    **What you accept** — the page is still very long. One URL means almost no search surface: _scaling humain_, _ere des femmes_ and _ecologie humaine_ can't rank separately. Nothing specific to send an ad to. And you can't tell which pillar people actually came for.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="B — Hub and pages">
    ## Hub and pages

    The homepage becomes a thin routing layer whose only job is sorting people into the right pillar. Each pillar becomes a real page that can rank, be linked, and be measured.

    |  |  |
    | --- | --- |
    | Effort | Medium — same copy, split across pages, nav rebuilt |
    | Best for | Making the site work as a business asset, not a brochure |
    | Time to live | Two to three weeks |

    ```text
      /                 hub - sorts and leaves
      /dirigeants       the money page, full ladder
      /diagnostic       NEW - the lead page
      /manifeste        NEW - its own URL
      /programmes       Lab + live programmes
      /ere-des-femmes
      /education
      /armelle          bio, books, press
      /conferences
      /contact
    ```

    The hub itself is short: hero, core message, **four full-width recognition bands** (one per pillar, each with a line of "this is you if..."), a proof strip, a manifesto teaser, and out.

    <Tip>
      Bands, not a four-up card grid. Cards make people compare. Bands make people recognise themselves — and recognition is what sorting needs.
    </Tip>

    **What you gain** — ads can point at a page instead of an anchor, which is what makes the whole ad strategy work. Each pillar ranks on its own terms. You finally learn which pillar people come for. The manifesto gets a shareable URL. And a CEO never has to scroll past _Redevenez Vivant_.

    **What you accept** — real build work. A thin homepage can feel underwhelming if the four bands aren't written well. More pages to keep current.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="C — Diagnostic-led">
    ## Diagnostic-led

    Open on the symptom, not the worldview. The visitor meets themselves in the first screen, takes a free self-scan, and only meets the philosophy once they're already in a conversation.

    |  |  |
    | --- | --- |
    | Effort | High — new copy, plus building the self-scan |
    | Best for | Maximum conversion from cold paid traffic |
    | Time to live | Four to six weeks |

    ```text
      hero            "Votre entreprise ne respire plus comme avant."
                      [ Faire le scan - gratuit, 5 min ]
      recognition     fatigue, decisions lentes, CODIR desaligne
      SELF-SCAN       NEW BUILD - 8-10 questions, scored map
      the reframe     ni coaching, ni conseil
      the ladder
      proof           case studies, press, books
      the worldview   demoted -> /vision
      close
    ```

    <Warning>
      **Read this before you like it.**

      This is the best-converting option and the least like you. We chose deliberately to lead with the worldview and monetise through HBA — precisely because a narrow business-first funnel is what made you feel misaligned in the first place. That is what HBA was, and you pulled away from it.

      Option C rebuilds a version of that. Three of your four pillars come off the main page. The manifesto gets demoted. It's here because it's genuinely the strongest commercial structure and you should see it — not because I'd recommend it. If you pick it, pick it knowing what it walks back toward.
    </Warning>

    **What you gain** — highest conversion from cold traffic by a wide margin, a genuinely defensible asset in the self-scan, and people qualified before they reach you.

    **What you accept** — the four-pillar house stops being visible. And it needs the case studies to be honest, which don't exist yet.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Side by side

|  | A — Reorder | B — Hub | C — Diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Build effort | Low | Medium | High |
| Holds the four-pillar house | Yes | Yes — best of the three | No |
| Somewhere to send an ad | Weak — anchors only | Strong | Strongest |
| Search surface | One URL | Ten URLs | Six URLs |
| Needs case studies | Helpful | Important | Required |
| Feels like you | Yes | Yes | Least |
| Time to live | Days | 2–3 weeks | 4–6 weeks |

## My recommendation

<Note>
  **B, arrived at through A.**

  **Do A this week**, because the June dates are live damage and the reorder is nearly free. That alone gets the offer above the fold.

  **Then build B**, because it's the only option that both keeps the four-pillar house intact and gives the ad strategy somewhere real to send traffic. A one-URL site can't support the advertising we've planned, and it can't tell you which pillar is actually working — which is the question everything else depends on.

  **And take one thing from C regardless: the self-scan.** It's the strongest single idea across all three options. It productises the top of your funnel, it's the missing free rung below the paid ladder, it's a real preview of the diagnostic, and no competitor can copy it because they _are_ their product. It works inside B without any of C's costs — it just lives at `/diagnostic` instead of taking over the homepage.
</Note>
