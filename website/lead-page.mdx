You said you want a lead page and don't know what to put on it. The answer is smaller than you think: **you already have one.**

The _"Etre avant d'etre entrepreneur"_ block is a lead page in miniature. It has the right asset (a mini-diagnostic — a preview of the actual product), the right audience, and a working capture form.

What it doesn't have is a URL of its own, or permission to follow up.

## So the lead page is that block, freed

On its own page, with the follow-up promise fixed, and the Meta ads pointed at it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Headline — the diagnostic promise">
    _Being, before entrepreneur._
  </Step>
  <Step title="Three or four lines on what the e-book actually asks you">
    This is the preview of the paid diagnostic. It should feel like a small version of the real thing, because it is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The form — email only">
    One field. Never more on a lead page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Two lines on who you are">
    Fifteen years, five books, the scaling-humain line. Not the full bio.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The honest promise">
    _No sales sequence. One idea a week. Leave whenever._
  </Step>
</Steps>

That's the whole page. It exists to do one thing.

## This is what closes the loop

```text
  show + ads
      |
      v
  lead page  ---->  e-book = preview of the diagnostic
      |
      v
  welcome sequence  ---->  sorts them into a pillar
      |
      v
  the diagnostic  ---->  the paid door
```

<Note>
  Every piece of this already exists. The only genuinely missing part was permission to email — which is why that one line on the e-book block matters more than it looks.
</Note>

## The upgrade, when you want it

Turn the e-book into a **scored self-scan**: eight to ten questions, a mapped result, emailed back.

It reads the same four things your paid diagnostic reads — the leader's posture, where the system's energy goes, the invisible tensions, the structural decisions — just self-served.

That gives you a free top rung below the paid ladder, a real destination for ads, and the one asset no competitor can copy, because they _are_ their product.
