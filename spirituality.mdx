Written after the 11 September call, in answer to a specific question. A sales trainer told you that no CEO would work with you if they knew about your spiritual path. She said it while holding a large spirituality of her own. So: is she right?

<Note>
  Short answer. She is describing a real constraint and drawing the wrong conclusion from it. The constraint is about **sequence**, not about concealment. Everyone who has built a large practice in this space is openly spiritual. None of them led with it.
</Note>

This is a scan of how the people who have already solved this problem solved it. It is a reading of public positioning, not a market study, and it is worth treating as a set of patterns rather than as evidence.

## Five patterns

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1. The business credential comes first, and it buys the permission" icon="badge-check">
    The pattern is almost uniform. Jerry Colonna's work is a blend of Buddhism, Jungian therapy and entrepreneurial straight talk, and it is sold under the name _radical self-inquiry_. What makes it sellable to founders is that he was a venture capitalist first. [Anna Yusim](https://annayusim.com/coaching/) markets to executives as a psychiatrist who also does spiritual work, in that order. Practitioners consistently lead with their business or clinical credentials and mention the deeper work second.

    The credential is not decoration. It is the thing that converts "this person is spiritual" from a risk into a differentiator.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="2. Translate to the operational problem, always" icon="languages">
    The guidance across the executive coaching market is consistent and blunt: corporate buyers are risk-averse, they are not funding a journey, and marketing has to translate soft work into the operational and strategic problems they are already trying to solve.

    This is precisely [the sidewalk rule](/messaging) you already run on, arrived at independently. Your vocabulary table is doing the same job that these practices do with positioning.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="3. Privacy is a feature, and it is sold as one" icon="lock">
    Several practices explicitly note that leaders choose to pay personally rather than through the company, to keep the work private.

    This is the president's-therapist point from the call, and it turns out to be standard practice rather than a hypothesis. It also changes what a €1,500 diagnostic is: a personally payable amount, invisible to the board, the investors and the team.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="4. The book is the channel, not the outreach" icon="book-open">
    The three people who moved this work furthest into companies all did it the same way. Frederic Laloux with _Reinventing Organizations_. Otto Scharmer with _Theory U_, which names an explicitly spiritual divide and still sits on corporate reading lists. Colonna with _Reboot_.

    Not one of them got there through cold messaging. They published a framework with a name, and the buyers came to it. Laloux went further and publicly offered non-spiritual re-framings of his own spiritual concepts, so that readers who could not accept the first version could still use the model.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="5. Groups de-risk what one-to-one exposes" icon="users-round">
    Colonna's stated formula is practical skills plus radical self-inquiry plus peer experience. [The Conscious Leadership Group](https://conscious.is/services/consulting) runs forums alongside consulting. The format is doing real work: a leader who will not book a private session about themselves will sit in a room where the subject is the topic rather than them.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The thing the sales trainer got wrong

She is right that a cold CEO, told about your spiritual path before anything else, will decline. That is genuinely how it goes, and your reply rate says so.

She is wrong that the answer is to hide it. The market she is describing already contains [a firm](https://conscious.is/) that states on its own site that it draws on consciousness, spirituality and psychology, and sells consulting to companies on that basis. The difference is not honesty. It is that they earned the right to say it before they said it.

<Info>
  Both things are true at once, and they are not in conflict:

  - **To a stranger**, the worldview is a liability. It is an unpriced risk attached to an unknown person.
  - **To someone who already trusts you**, it is the entire differentiator. It is the reason you see what the last three advisors missed.

  Concealment is not the strategy. Sequence is.
</Info>

## What this means for you

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Do more of" icon="circle-check">
    **Lead with the record.** Six diagnostics, [three documented cases](/proof), the books, fifteen years of studying human systems, and a show whose next guests are the former Michelin General Director and the founder of BlaBlaCar. That is a serious credential stack and it is currently arriving after the worldview rather than before it.

    **Finish the book.** On this evidence it is your highest-leverage asset by some distance, and it is already in progress.

    **Build the webinar series.** The three-part series you decided on is the right format, for the reason in pattern 5. Make the subject the defence mechanism, not the attendee.

    **Offer the private, personally paid route.** Say out loud that the diagnostic can be paid personally and stays outside the company. For a defended leader that removes the actual objection, which was never about money.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Do less of" icon="circle-x">
    **Cold outreach as the main engine.** Nobody in this space built a practice this way. Keep it running as a test bed (that is what [the two plays](/outreach) are for) and stop treating its failure as a verdict on the positioning.

    **Importing the US vocabulary.** _Conscious leadership_ is an established category in English. It does not carry to a French SME leader, and the local acceptable register is nearer to _entreprise libérée_ and the management-renewal conversation. Your own words already work better than the imported ones.

    **Arguing with the objection.** The sales trainer's reaction is the objection itself, in person. It is answered with a case study, not with a defence.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## One reframe worth keeping

Scharmer's work sells to corporations while explicitly naming an ecological, a social and a spiritual divide. Laloux's sells while explicitly building on stages of human consciousness. Neither hides. What both do is give the buyer a **model with a name** that can be discussed in a meeting without anyone having to say anything about themselves.

<Note>
  That is what HBA already is, and it may be undersold. A named method is a socially safe object. A leader can commission a Human Business Architecture diagnostic and describe it to their board as an organisational review, entirely truthfully, without ever having to explain where it came from.

  The method is the permission structure. It is doing more work than the deck currently gives it credit for.
</Note>

## What would change this reading

<Warning>
  This is a scan of public positioning, and it has real limits.

  - Everything here is English-language and mostly US. The French market for this is smaller, more relational and more sceptical, and none of these practices are evidence about it.
  - The named practitioners all have large audiences already. What works at their scale is not automatically what creates the first ten clients.
  - No pricing, conversion or channel data is public for any of them. This is reasoning from how they present themselves.

  The two live speaking engagements will tell you more about the French room in a fortnight than any of this does.
</Warning>
