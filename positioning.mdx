## The master statement

This is the refined version, broadened as we agreed on 21 July. The old version said "from a company to a family," which quietly capped the idea at two examples. The new one doesn't:

<Note>
  Human Ecology is a new design for society. It sees every human system as a living ecosystem that only does well when everything and everyone is in its right place. Armelle Six helps leaders find that right place, so their organisation can grow without losing its humanity, or theirs.
</Note>

What this is for. It is an alignment statement, not a slogan. You will probably never say it out loud in these exact words. Its job is to be the tuning fork. When a stranger describes what you do, we want their description to land as close to this as possible. If it does, the brand is working.

How to use it in practice. Say the shape, not the sentence. In conversation this usually comes out as:

> "I look at companies the way you'd look at a living ecosystem. When something isn't in its right place, the whole system feels it."

## The four pillars, each with its own statement

You asked whether each pillar should have its own. Yes. Same essence, different door. Use the one that matches the room you're in.

| Pillar | Positioning statement |
| --- | --- |
| **Omara Soma** | The source. For people who feel the disconnection in themselves first, and want to come back to what is already there rather than add something new. |
| **The Era of Women** | For women who are done asking for a place, and are ready to take the one that is theirs, then write new rules once they're standing in it. |
| **HBA** | For leaders whose company has outgrown the way it was built. A diagnostic that finds where the human system is out of place, and a method to put it back, so the company can grow without breaking the people inside it. |
| **Education** | For parents and educators who want to raise humans who never lose their right place to begin with. |

<Warning>
  On the Era of Women. Your framing is "step into your place, take it, create new rules," not the fight-for-a-seat framing. Keep it that way. It is more distinctive, and it is what you actually believe.
</Warning>

## The name: we keep it

Romain Cristofini's practice uses "écologie humaine et essentielle." You are L'Écologie Humaine (lecologiehumaine.com). We considered renaming and decided to keep the name. It is yours, it is the mother-concept the whole house hangs from, and changing it to avoid a competitor would be the same compromise that produced HBA in the first place.

That decision is settled. What it obligates us to do is not, so here are the four rules that follow from keeping it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Own la juste place as the concept, not the category name">
    "Écologie humaine" is now contested ground, and no amount of copy will win it outright. _La juste place_ is uncontested, it is yours, and it is more useful anyway: a specific idea rather than a category label. In practice, the name goes on the door, but _la juste place_ is what shows up in the writing, the diagnostic, the offer names and the site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Don't fight on his keywords">
    He holds "leaders éclairés," "intelligence spirituelle" and "leadership régénératif," and he has a 4,700-member community feeding them. Chasing them means competing at his strength with none of his volume. Build instead on the language that is already distinctly yours: _juste place, système vivant, nouveau design de société, grandir sans se perdre_.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make un nouveau design de société carry the differentiation">
    It's already your show title, and it is the sharper end of the brand. It says what the ecology is for. Where the name overlaps, the tagline separates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secure the surface">
    Before any site work, confirm the domain and check the handle situation across LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram and Facebook. Since we're keeping a contested name, consistency across every surface is what makes you findable rather than confusable. Worth checking whether "L'Écologie Humaine" is registrable as a mark in your classes. If it is, that closes the question permanently.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The upside of this decision: you keep the concept the entire house rests on, and you avoid the thing that broke the last version of this brand.

The cost we accept: shared search ground with your nearest competitor. Rules 1 to 3 are how we manage it.

</Info>

## One phrase worth promoting

"Scaling humain," currently buried in your site's job title, is the most commercially precise phrase you own. It's business-native, it says what you do, no competitor uses it, and it translates the whole worldview into two words a founder already understands.

It is stronger than "écologie humaine" as a commercial handle, which matters given the name decision above. Promote it from a job title to a working term.
