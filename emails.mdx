You have a list of roughly 10,000 people. That is your most valuable asset and it is currently used for book launches only. These three sequences change that.

<Warning>
  One conflict to resolve first. Your site's e-book block currently promises "Aucune liste de diffusion. Pas de relances commerciales," meaning no mailing list and no follow-up.

  That promise and this page cannot both stand. Right now your only lead-capture mechanism is formally forbidden from ever contacting anyone.

  The [website deliverable](/website) proposes replacing it with "No sales sequence. One idea a week, and you can leave whenever you want," which keeps the honesty and opens the door. If you'd rather keep the strict version, say so and this whole page gets rebuilt around the show and the existing 10,000 only. It's a real choice. Make it deliberately.
</Warning>

## Rules for all of them

- One idea per email
- Short paragraphs
- Never open at [level 4](/messaging)
- Sign as yourself
- One link, one ask

## Welcome sequence: 5 emails

For new subscribers, and worth re-running once to the existing 10,000 as a re-introduction, since most of them met you as a spiritual teacher and don't know about the rest.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Email 1, send immediately">
    Subject: _What I see when I look at a company_

    Open on the hook. Tell them, in four or five lines, that you spent fifteen years studying how human systems work, and that you now bring that into companies. Say what they'll get from you: one idea a week, no lesson.

    No ask.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Email 2, day 3">
    Subject: _The problem is almost never the person_

    The diagnosis, level 2. One concrete situation: a team conflict that turned out to be a placement problem. This is the email that makes them feel understood.

    Ask: reply and tell me where you feel it in your own organisation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Email 3, day 7">
    Subject: _The house_

    Explain the four pillars, plainly, and say which door is which. This is your sorting email. It does the work of [the self-sorting map](/audiences).

    Ask: which of these four is yours?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Email 4, day 12">
    Subject: _Why I'm not a consultant_

    [The reversal](/objections), told as a story rather than a claim. Soften the confrontation into narrative. You're not attacking anyone, you're explaining where you come from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Email 5, day 18">
    Subject: _So what now?_

    The one offer email. Name HBA specifically, say who it's for, and give one clear next step. Everything before this was giving.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Show follow-up: a weekly template, not a sequence

You publish weekly. Every episode should produce one email, sent 24 hours after, to the whole list. Same four-block shape every time, so it takes you fifteen minutes:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="1. The one line" icon="quote">
    The single line that mattered most in the episode.
  </Card>

  <Card title="2. Two or three sentences" icon="align-left">
    What came up, and why it matters to them.
  </Card>

  <Card title="3. The link" icon="link">
    To the full episode.
  </Card>

  <Card title="4. One question back" icon="message-circle">
    Replies are what keep you out of promotions tabs.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Note>
  Of everything in this pack, this is the habit that pays back the most. The show already has energy. The list already exists. Nothing currently connects them. Fifteen minutes a week, every Tuesday. See [The weekly rhythm](/rhythm).
</Note>

## HBA discovery sequence: 4 emails

For someone who has had a first conversation but hasn't signed.

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| **Day 1** | Recap what you saw in their system. Specific. This email is the demonstration. |
| **Day 4** | One case, one before and after. Blocked on the case studies. |
| **Day 9** | Answer the objection you heard on the call, directly, using [Objection handling](/objections). |
| **Day 16** | The clean close: "Is this the right moment, or not yet? Either answer works for me." |

<Info>
  That last one gives them permission to say no. It converts better, and it's more like you.
</Info>
