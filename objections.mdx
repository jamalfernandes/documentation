For each one: what they actually mean, and how you answer. **Answer the meaning, not the words.**

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="You've never run a company." icon="briefcase">
    _The real question: can you possibly understand my reality?_

    Don't apologise for it, and don't oversell a business CV you don't have. Turn it:

    > "That's true. I haven't run your company, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. What I did for fifteen years was study how human systems work — and a company is one. Most of the people advising you are former executives who found inner work along the way. I came from the other side. I'm what they went looking for."

    Then move immediately to a diagnostic question about _their_ company. The proof is that you see something they hadn't.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="This sounds spiritual / esoteric — and its French cousin, derive sectaire." icon="triangle-exclamation">
    _The real question: is this safe for me professionally?_

    France's corporate risk radar is trained on exactly your profile. Never argue with the fear — outrun it with structure:

    > "It's a fair question. What I offer is a diagnostic with a method, stages and a deliverable. There are four published books behind it, a named framework, clear pricing. If at the end you haven't seen something you couldn't see before, it didn't work."

    Your four structural answers, always available: **a named method**, **published books**, **clear offers and pricing**, **real client testimonials**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="We already have a coach / consultant / HR provider." icon="users">
    _The real question: what's different about you?_

    > "Good — and I don't do the same thing. A coach works with a person. A consultant works on the organisation. I look at the complete human system: who is in which place, and where it's binding. That's usually the level nobody looks at, because it doesn't belong to any one function."
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What's the ROI?" icon="chart-line">
    _The real question: I need to justify this to myself or to a board._

    Don't invent numbers. Point at costs they already carry:

    > "Look at what misalignment is already costing you: turnover, failed hires, the meetings that keep repeating, the time you spend putting out fires instead of building. I'm not selling an improvement. I'm showing you where the system is losing its energy, and what that costs."

    Once you have the three case studies, this answer gets a number attached to it. Until then it stays structural.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="We don't have time right now." icon="clock">
    _The real question: this isn't urgent enough._

    > "That's often the symptom itself. A leader's lack of time is rarely a scheduling problem — it's a placement problem. The diagnostic takes \[X\]. It's short on purpose."
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It's too expensive." icon="tag">
    _The real question: I don't yet see the value clearly enough._

    Never discount to close. Reduce scope instead. And use the doctor question:

    > "Would you rather go to a doctor who's famous, or a doctor whose patients leave healthy?"
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can you do this in English?" icon="globe">
    Yes — and it's an opening rather than a problem. The show auto-translates on YouTube, your books can be AI-translated, and the English-language market for this is less crowded and less allergic to the vocabulary than the French one.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
