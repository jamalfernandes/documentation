Source: _Human Business Architecture, trois cas documentés_, July 2026. Six HBA diagnostics have been run to date. Three have enough follow-up to document what the diagnostic set in motion.

<Note>
  The common thread, and it is the whole argument: in all three cases the difficulty as stated was not the cause. The diagnostic revealed a question of place, leadership or architecture that was organising the visible symptoms.
</Note>

## The three at a glance

| Case | Problem as presented | What moved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Artisanal cheesemaker**, about 20 staff | Unfinished leadership handover | Leadership reclaimed, collective calm restored, governance work opened |
| **2. Water-sector SME** | Hypercontrolling employee, dismissal being considered | Frame restored, employee back inside their own scope, conflicts subsided |
| **3. Printworks**, 18 staff | Exhausted leader wanting to buy other companies | Order of growth clarified, first hires made, reorganisation under way |

## Case 1: the artisanal cheesemaker

_When a leader is slow to take their place, the whole company wavers._

A family business of around twenty people. The new owner had been taking over progressively, but the handover from the former leader had never actually completed. Both still partly occupied the same place.

**Symptoms.** Revenue starting to fall, internal tension rising, departures multiplying, several staff exhausted or off with burnout, and outside partners no longer quite respecting the company's frame, as though it had lost its centre of gravity.

**What the diagnostic revealed.** Not a shortage of procedures or skills. An absence of handover. When the leader's place is unclear, responsibilities blur, tension rises, and the system slowly loses its ability to work.

<Note>
  The company did not need more procedures. It needed its leader to fully retake their right place.
</Note>

**What actually happened.** The former leader was repositioned onto a scope that fitted. Teams began to breathe and the climate eased. Staff regained stability and confidence, decisions became legible, and a first core group was formed to prepare the handover of the historic craft knowledge.

The diagnostic also surfaced a lever previously treated as secondary: vision and culture. Two days after the restitution, the leader gathered everyone at the summer barbecue and asked Armelle to run a short talk and a collective workshop. In a company where recent conflicts had made group meetings liable to catch fire, the exchange stayed calm, warm and constructive, and staff requests turned into concrete governance topics.

**Status.** Conflict resolution is still in progress. But the system has changed dynamic, and issues once lived as interpersonal tension are now handled as questions of leadership, organisation and governance.

## Case 2: the water-sector SME

_When an employee takes too much space because the leadership function has been left vacant._

The leader had a serious problem with an employee whose behaviour had become extremely controlling toward colleagues. In his own words, the employee had already contributed to one colleague's dismissal and was now going after another. Dismissal was being seriously considered.

**What makes this case valuable:** the employee and the leader had already been through coaching on exactly this situation, with no change.

**What the diagnostic revealed.** The leadership function had been vacant since the co-founder died some years earlier. That co-founder had hired the employee and had carried all of the leadership and management. The remaining leader saw himself as an engineer and technician, loved the craft, and did not consider himself a leader or a manager. He confirmed his difficulty saying no, setting a frame, holding limits and giving clear direction.

With no leadership clearly held, an essential place stayed empty. The employee progressively filled it. Someone easily made to feel insecure, meeting no solid frame, may try to manufacture their own security through control.

<Note>
  The employee's behaviour was real. But treating it as the sole origin of the problem prevented anyone from seeing the architecture feeding it.
</Note>

**What actually happened.** Two in-depth interviews of about an hour, focused on the leader retaking his place rather than on another attempt to correct the employee. In the following weeks, as he set more frame and gave clearer direction, the controlling behaviour reduced, the employee returned to their proper function, tension with colleagues eased, apparently insoluble conflicts subsided, and the dismissal was dropped.

## Case 3: the printworks

_Before buying other companies, build the architecture that can hold them._

Eighteen staff. A former salesperson in the business who was offered the company when the owner left. He runs it well and carries a larger ambition: buy several other printworks and build a group. At the time of the diagnostic he could not decide whether to move.

**The situation.** Exhausted and stretched thin, still personally carrying much of the sales function on top of running the company. Little support at his level. Overlapping roles. Some staff without enough work but still paid. Vision and culture no longer structuring how the company worked.

Underneath: a fragile sense of legitimacy, because he had not arrived "through the front door." He disliked conflict, struggled to say no, and wanted to be liked by everyone. He was also paying some staff more than himself, earning less than when he was an employee there.

**What the diagnostic revealed.** His first question was whether to buy other printworks. The diagnostic confirmed the vision was sound, and that the order of development had to be respected. The problem was not the ambition. It was the timing, and whether the current architecture could carry it.

<Note>
  The growth vision is right. The system has to build the human, managerial and financial architecture capable of carrying it first.
</Note>

**What actually happened in the two months after.** The employee he had been walking on eggshells around left of her own accord, three to four weeks after the diagnostic. He recruited a salesperson, accepting he could no longer carry that function alone. He promoted several younger staff with real potential and began rebuilding support around himself. And he recovered a clearer view of the steps to take before considering acquisitions.

A structured implementation programme started two months later: a day on the org chart, roles and each person's right place; a half-day on vision and culture; two 90-minute sessions on legitimacy, leadership posture and the relationship to money; an integration session; and a session on the tensions emerging as younger staff take on more responsibility.

## What these three let you say

Claims that are factually supported by the cases:

- The HBA diagnostic has already triggered concrete changes in leadership, organisation and governance.
- In a situation where dismissal was being considered despite prior coaching, repositioning the leader let the employee recover their role and the conflicts subside.
- In a company under heavy tension, the intervention that came out of the diagnostic produced a calm, constructive collective dialogue, followed by governance decisions.
- In a company weighing several acquisitions, the diagnostic confirmed the vision while identifying the human, organisational and financial groundwork its success depended on.

Three things they establish, which map directly onto the [messaging levels](/messaging):

1. A targeted diagnostic can be enough to shift a relational dynamic that had become unbearable.
2. Repositioning a leader can restore the frame without having to remove the person originally named as the problem.
3. A growth vision can be sound and still require the system to be reorganised before it can carry it.

## Limits on how these can be used

<Warning>
  These come from Armelle's own document and are not negotiable in anything published.

  - Do not present the three companies as fully transformed.
  - Do not announce financial results or return on investment that have not been measured.
  - Do not claim that all conflicts are definitively resolved.
  - Distinguish between changes already made, decisions taken, and work still under way.
  - Keep companies and people anonymised in all external communication.
</Warning>

<Info>
  This is why [the ROI answer](/objections) still does not get a number attached to it. The cases unlock proof of _movement_, not proof of return. Anyone asking for a figure gets the structural answer plus a case, not an invented percentage.
</Info>
