You have a list of roughly 10,000 people. It is your single most valuable asset, and it is currently used for book launches only. These three sequences change that.

<Warning>
  **One conflict to resolve first.** Your site's e-book block currently promises _"Aucune liste de diffusion. Pas de relances commerciales"_ — no mailing list, no follow-up. That promise and this page cannot both stand: right now your only lead-capture mechanism is formally forbidden from ever contacting anyone.

  The website work proposes replacing it with _"No sales sequence. One idea a week, and you can leave whenever you want"_ — which keeps the honesty and opens the door. If you'd rather keep the strict version, say so and this whole page gets rebuilt around the show and the existing list only. It's a real choice; make it deliberately.
</Warning>

<Note>
  **Rules for all three.** One idea per email. Short paragraphs. Never open at level 4. Sign as yourself. One link, one ask.
</Note>

## 1. Welcome sequence — five emails

For new subscribers. Also worth running once to the existing 10,000 as a re-introduction, since most of them met you as a spiritual teacher and don't know about the rest.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send immediately — What I see when I look at a company">
    Open on the hook. Four or five lines: you spent fifteen years studying how human systems work, and you now bring that into companies. Say what they'll get from you — one idea a week, no lesson. **No ask.**
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day 3 — The problem is almost never the person">
    The diagnosis, level 2. One concrete situation: a team conflict that turned out to be a placement problem. This is the email that makes them feel understood. Ask them to reply with where they feel it in their own organisation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day 7 — The house">
    The four pillars, plainly, and which door is which. This is your sorting email. Ask: which of these four is yours?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day 12 — Why I'm not a consultant">
    The reversal, told as a story rather than a claim. Soften the confrontation into narrative — you're not attacking anyone, you're explaining where you come from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day 18 — So what now?">
    The one offer email. Name HBA specifically, say who it's for, give one clear next step. Everything before this was giving.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 2. Show follow-up — a weekly template

Not a sequence. You publish weekly; every episode should produce one email, sent 24 hours after, to the whole list. Same four-block shape every time, so it takes fifteen minutes:

1. **The one line** that mattered most in the episode
2. **Two or three sentences** on what came up and why it matters to them
3. **The link** to the full episode
4. **One question** back to them — replies are what keep you out of promotions tabs

<Tip>
  This is the highest-leverage habit in the whole pack. The show already has energy. The list already exists. Nothing currently connects them.
</Tip>

## 3. HBA discovery sequence — four emails

For someone who has had a first conversation but hasn't signed.

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Recap what you saw in _their_ system. Specific. This email is the demonstration |
| Day 4 | One case, one before-and-after — _blocked on the case studies_ |
| Day 9 | Answer the objection you actually heard on the call |
| Day 16 | The clean close: _"Is this the right moment, or not yet? Either answer works for me."_ |

That last one gives them permission to say no. It converts better, and it's more like you.
