## What we actually know

| Signal | Number | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct CEO messages, vague framing | Near zero replies | The message was doing the sorting, and it sorted everyone out |
| Direct CEO messages, blunt framing ("this is what I do, are you interested, shall we talk") | Roughly 3% reply, and the replies are no | The message works. The list is wrong |
| Messages to consulting firms offering a complementary service | No replies | Cold partnership pitches do not land without a relationship |
| Inbound connection requests | Mostly coaches and therapists | There is real demand here. It is just not the demand we asked for |
| CEO connection requests | Only from the Boost33 campaign | Unassisted, CEOs are not finding you |

<Note>
  Read the 3% correctly. A blunt message that gets a 3% reply rate on a cold, unqualified list is not a failed message. It is a working message pointed at the wrong people. The agency is now fixing the list. These two plays fix everything the agency is not touching.
</Note>

<Warning>
  One live debt worth collecting before any new outreach. Your one current CEO client agreed to make an introduction and has not made it. That is the single warmest lead you own, it costs nothing, and it is the only referral in the system. Ask him directly, by name, for one name.
</Warning>

## Play A: the slow game

For CEOs. Built on a simple premise: you cannot ask a defended person for a call before they have ever spoken to you. So stop asking. Ask a question instead.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect with no pitch">
    Connection request, no note, or a one-line note that names nothing commercial. Any pitch in the request costs you the connection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send one question, every two weeks">
    One short question. Multiple choice, so answering costs a single letter. No link, no offer, no follow-up if they ignore it. Same cadence for everyone on the list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let them sort themselves">
    Most will never answer. Fine. The ones who answer two or three times have selected themselves, and their answers tell you what is wrong in their company before you have spent a minute on them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn, once, after three answers">
    Not a pitch. An observation, based on what they told you, plus one thing they could try. The call is offered as an option they can decline without cost.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop at six unanswered">
    Twelve weeks of silence means no. Remove them and stop. This keeps the play from becoming harassment, which is the one thing that would burn the profile.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### The question bank

Eight questions, one per fortnight, in this order. Each is [level 1 or 2](/messaging), and each quietly reads a different part of the system.

| # | The question | What the answer tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | When something goes wrong in your company, what is your first instinct? **(a)** look at the person **(b)** look at the process **(c)** look at yourself | (c) is an aware leader. (a) is the profile the agency is now filtering out |
| 2 | Which costs you more energy right now? **(a)** the work **(b)** the people **(c)** the decisions | (b) and (c) are yours. (a) is not a client yet |
| 3 | In the last year, has your company gained clarity or gained complexity? **(a)** clarity **(b)** complexity **(c)** both, in different places | Complexity is [hook 12](/messaging). This opens the placement conversation |
| 4 | Is there someone in your company doing a job that is not really theirs? **(a)** yes **(b)** no **(c)** yes, and it is me | (c) is the highest-intent answer in the whole bank |
| 5 | When you grew, what got harder that you did not expect? | Open question, deliberately. By now they have answered three times and will write a sentence |
| 6 | Who do you talk to about the parts of the job you cannot say out loud? **(a)** co-founder or partner **(b)** a coach or advisor **(c)** nobody | (c) is isolation, which is [hook 13](/messaging), and it is the strongest buying signal you will get |
| 7 | If you could put one thing back where it belongs, what would it be? | The whole method, asked as a question. Their answer is your opening line on the call |
| 8 | Do you feel like the leader of your company, or like the person running it? | The tightest question you have. The [three cases](/proof) are all people who answered the second half |

<Info>
  Question 8 is the one to watch. All three documented cases are leaders who were running the company without feeling like its leader. If that answer keeps repeating in the outreach too, it stops being an observation and becomes the targeting instruction.
</Info>

### The turn message

Send once, to anyone who answered three or more times. One template, filled with their own words.

<Note>
  I have been asking you a question every couple of weeks, and you have been generous enough to answer. So here is what I noticed, for whatever it is worth.

  You said **[their answer to Q4 or Q6]**. In my experience that is almost never a people problem. It is usually a sign that a place in the company is unclear, and that someone has quietly moved into it.

  One thing worth trying: **[one concrete, free suggestion tied to their answer]**.

  I might be reading it wrong, I only have three answers to go on. If it is close and you want to talk it through, I am happy to. If not, I will keep sending questions.
</Note>

Why this converts better than a pitch. They have already spoken to you eight times. You are demonstrating the method rather than describing it, which is [the sidewalk rule](/messaging) working exactly as intended. And the last line removes the cost of saying no, which is the same move that closes [the discovery sequence](/emails).

## Play B: bottom-up, through the coaches

Coaches and therapists are the people actually asking to connect with you. Until now that has been treated as noise. It is the second play.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Why it works" icon="circle-check">
    They already have the CEOs you cannot reach. They sit with them weekly. They hear the company problems and have no way to act on them, because they work at the individual level and the problem is structural.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Why they will not steal it" icon="shield-check">
    They cannot do what you do. HBA reads the system through the leader and repositions places. That is not a coaching skill, and [case 2](/proof) is the proof: the employee and the leader had both already been coached on exactly that situation, with no change.
  </Card>
</Columns>

### The message to a coach who connects

<Note>
  Thanks for connecting. Out of curiosity, since you work with leaders too: how often do you end up in a session where the thing blocking your client is not actually in your client? Where it is the structure around them, and there is nothing either of you can do about it from inside the session?

  That gap is basically what I work on. Happy to swap notes if it is useful.
</Note>

That is it. No offer, no partner programme, no deck. The question is the qualifier: a coach who has never had that experience is not your person, and a coach who has will answer at length.

### What the relationship becomes

<Steps>
  <Step title="Swap notes, once">
    Thirty minutes. You are not selling. You are finding out what they keep hitting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give them the language">
    Send them the one thing they can use immediately: the distinction between a person problem and a place problem. Give it away. It makes them better at their job and it names you as the source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the referral trade explicit and two-way">
    You will meet leaders who need individual work you do not do. Send those to them. A referral loop that only runs one way dies in a month.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stay in their head">
    They do not need to sell you. They need your name to surface the next time a client describes a structural problem. One useful message a quarter is enough.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  The trust move here, and it is the whole thing: a coach who sends their client elsewhere looks better to that client, not worse. Say that out loud when you make the trade. It is why the good ones will play.
</Warning>

## The direct-message test

The agency owns paid targeting. You still own your own outbox. Run four variants against comparable lists and keep the numbers, because nothing in this account has been measured per-message yet.

| Variant | Shape | Hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Blunt** | The current one. What I do, I know you are busy, are you interested, shall we talk | The 3% baseline. Everything is measured against this |
| **B. Symptom-first** | Open on a symptom they have, not on what you do. "Most leaders I speak to at your size have one person quietly doing a job that is not theirs." Then one line on you | Recognition beats description |
| **C. Question-only** | Question 1 or 4 from the bank, sent cold, with no pitch at all | Tests whether the slow game works without the connection warm-up. Cheapest variant to run |
| **D. Proof-first** | One anonymised case in three lines, ending with "if that sounds familiar, I am happy to talk" | Tests whether [the cases](/proof) do the selling. Keep to the anonymisation rules |

<Info>
  Twenty-five messages per variant, minimum, before you read anything into the result. Log reply rate and positive-reply rate separately. A "no" is still a reply and still data, which is the only reason we know variant A works at all.
</Info>
